Predicting where a wildfire goes next, with a lighter model and an honest error bar.

A lightweight linear-attention U-Net forecasts next-day fire spread from satellite and weather data, then turns its own uncertainty into a four-level risk triage a fire-watch coordinator can act on.

Dataset: NDWS (Huot et al., 2022)Task: next-day fire mask, 64 × 64 patchesModels: Attention U-Net vs. Lightweight U-Net

How it works, and where it falls short

20 MC-Dropout passesdropout left on at test time
Mean and spreadper pixel: prediction and uncertainty
Risk tier + adviceLow, Moderate, High, Extreme
  • One dataset (contiguous United States). Generalisation to other regions is untested.
  • Absolute accuracy is modest and varies between training runs; the two models are best described as comparable.
  • FLOPs are counted with thop (convolutions, linear layers, batch-norm). GPU latency is near-identical at this input size.
  • Risk thresholds are calibrated on a validation split, not against real operational outcomes.
  • Decision support only. Not an autonomous evacuation or dispatch system.
